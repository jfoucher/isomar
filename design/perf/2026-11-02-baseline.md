# Performance pass 2, 2026-11-02: panning, zooming, routing, best departure

Baseline measured on commit 06ea861 (0.4.0), before the changes of this pass; "after" columns once they were made.

## How it was measured

- **Machine:** the owner's Framework laptop, Ryzen 7840U, 8 cores / 16 threads, Fedora.
- **Render:**
  - `scripts/bench.sh`: a headless GNOME Shell at the owner's panel size (2256×1504, window 2256×1450), a copy of the owner's settings (text 150%, `online-depth` on, the free-charts folder), `ISOMAR_OFFLINE`, `ISOMAR_PALETTE=day`, 15 s to settle, then `ISOMAR_BENCH` (60 steps at 60 Hz: pan 20+5 px a step, zoom ±0.05 a step).
  - Caches warmed first with `scripts/bench.sh --warm`.
  - CPU is the figure that matters (battery on board).
  - `--timing` adds `scripts/bench-layers.py`'s count of tiles drawn and time per layer, and why tiles were drawn again (`ISOMAR_DEBUG_TIMING` logs `redrawn z/x/y for <layers>`, `*` where a layer changed everywhere).
- **Routing:**
  - `cargo run --release -p isomar-core --example routebench -- R11|R12|R14 [route|window|both]` replays `flows::routing::run` and `flows::departures::run`.
  - It uses the cached land tiles and the forecast frozen in `testdata/bench-weather` (a copy of `~/.cache/isomar/weather` taken that day, 984 files, runs to 2026110206), `BENCH_NOW=2026-11-02T12:00:00Z`, the boat's `polar.csv`, `Weights::comfortable`, 3 days at 3 h steps (25 departures).
  - Its `result` line is a checksum: arrival or stopping point, best departure, the ten best costs.
- **Profiles:**
  - `scripts/profile.sh` (eu-stack sampling; perf is not installed). Without inline frames eu-stack takes 0.16 s a sample on the GTK app, 1.7 s with them.
  - Build with `CARGO_PROFILE_RELEASE_DEBUG=line-tables-only CARGO_TARGET_DIR=target/prof cargo build --release`.

Found:
- **Tiles drawn again for nothing.** In a quiet pan at Iroise 107 distinct tiles were drawn, 64 of which had been drawn before the pan with nothing new arriving since (Loire: 56 of 106). At Boston every tile was drawn 2.6× during the pan.
- **Start-up.** Each of the 80 tiles in view was drawn 5× (Iroise), 7.7× (Loire) and 5.5× (Boston) while the data loaded.
- **Cause of the redraws in France** (`redrawn … for bsb`): when a pan shows land tiles not yet read, `LandLayer::covers_view` is false for a moment. `BsbLayer` then switches to the rasters that do not suit the base map, decodes the 1:10M pilot chart NAC01 (−100…33°E, 5…67°N), and bumps its bounds, so every tile is drawn again under it.
- **A raster drawn overzoomed in France** (not fixed in this pass, the owner's decision). At Loire and Brest that pilot chart stays over the z13 view as coloured blocks and hides the land. With `ISOMAR_HIDE=bsb` the Loire pan costs 0.78 s instead of 3.3 s.
- **Cause at Boston:**
  - `TownLayer::add_tile` bumped everywhere for every tile fetched for a water's shape (41 times while starting).
  - ENC cells, the aids' exclusions and the towns that follow them bumped about every 0.5 s while cells were read.

Profile of the Boston pan, tile workers (`EncPaint::paint` 86%):
- **`ReliefPaint::paint` 58%.** `draw_cell` called it for each drawn cell, and each call sampled, smoothed and shaded the whole tile and ran its contours before clipping to that cell's land; land and towns painted it again.
- Within relief, `Mosaic::height` 40%: SipHash `HashMap<TileId>` lookups for four pixels per sample (17%), and Mercator per sample.
- `enc::trace` → `geo::project` 19%: each point projected, plus the view centre projected again and `exp2` for the world size, on every point.
- cairo strokes 16%.

Profile of an R11 explore, one thread:
- **42%** in the `reach` score: `SeaField::nearest` scanned its 5×5 window before trying the point's own cell, computing about 12 haversines a lookup. glibc's rounding-mode save in trig alone was 35% of self time.

  - `blocked` 18%, walking every leg with two libm `floor` calls a step.
  - wind 14%, half of it sampling the wind at each new point only to see that there is some.
  - the polar 4%.
- Nearly every candidate went into a cell an earlier front had reached, and was dropped only after paying for all of that.

## What changed

Router (`isomar-core/src/routing/`):
- `SeaField::nearest` tries the point's own cell first and measures flat within a few cells.
- `explore` leaves out legs into reached cells before asking about land, wind and fields, but keeps each node's nearest such leg, and `longest_leg` is over the whole fan.
- `RasterLand` keeps 8×8 blocks of "any land"; its walk has no `floor` calls.
- `DetailedLand` keeps its sea fields.
- `Environment::has_wind`.
- `Polar::at_wind`, `LatLon::destination_by`, the fan's sines by angle addition, a cheap hash for cells.
- Larger rayon tasks.
- `Scan::kept`, with `flows::routing::show_search` to preview a kept search.

Render (`isomar-app`):
- `ReliefPaint` works a tile's relief out once, as a picture every caller paints under its clip.
- `Mosaic::heights` for a whole lattice.
- `ViewInfo::projector` for `enc::trace`.
- `BsbLayer` waits 1.5 s before a lapse of the base map's coverage changes its charts.
- Towns bump only the waters' area for a tile fetched for a water's shape.
- Stale tiles wait up to 1.5 s while a layer is `loading` (ENC reads, folder index).
- `ISOMAR_DEBUG_TIMING` logs why tiles are drawn again.

Not done:
- The overzoomed pilot chart in France (a separate task).
- Boston's ENC tiles are still 35 ms each. In the profile after the changes:
  - `enc::trace` is 27% of a tile, about half of it the Mercator of each point;
  - `chartinfo::draw_areas` 22% and the zone-of-confidence strokes 8% (cairo strokes 30% in all);
  - `plan_names` 10%, the land layer 11%, the relief only 8% (was 58%).
  - `features_in` still scans every feature of every cell twice per tile.
- Pre-projected ENC geometry (B5 of the plan) is the next step for `trace`; cutting the dashed area outlines to the tile before stroking, for `draw_areas`.
- A single route still spins rayon threads: 16 threads use about twice the CPU of one thread for 3.3× the speed.
