Why Isomar

Passage planning on a laptop is harder than it should be.

Isomar started from the frustrations of planning real passages on a laptop.

Obsolete chart plottersCramped toolbars, tiny dialogs and frequent crashes.

A native app on every screen. Linux, Mac, iPhone, iPad and Android, with smooth panning, zooming, and everything one click or tap from the chart.

Huge GRIB files over slow 4GA whole ocean of forecast when you just need a single bay.

Only what you need. Pick the area and check the size before you download. A coastal forecast is a few hundred KB.

Hard to tell when to leave portA router gives you a line, then you guess the day.

Best departure date. Isomar routes every departure in a window and ranks them by hours upwind, wind, waves and darkness.

Charts locked to one deviceSubscriptions, dongles, formats you can't open.

Open formats you own. Plain S-57 and KAP charts, a free worldwide planning chart, GPX in and out.

Charts

Clear charts

Other plotters often hide names under symbols and show everything at the same size.

Isomar tries to get close to the feeling of a paper chart. Sea names spread across the water, marks and lights thin out as you zoom out so the chart stays readable, and shading of the land helps recognise landmarks.

  • S-57 vector charts with their updates, drawn in a clear S-52 style, and BSB/KAP rasters.
  • A free planning chart anywhere: OpenStreetMap coast and seamarks, land relief, town names, and sounding depths (in Europe).
  • Click anything (a buoy, a wreck, a cable, a restricted area) to get information about the feature.
  • Day, dusk and night palettes that keep your night vision.

Weather

Faster forecasts download on slow or expensive connections

GRIB downloads are big, and it is hard to know which model and fields to pick, or how big the file will be on a phone hotspot.

Right-click the chart and choose Get weather here, for this view or for an area. Isomar asks NOAA's GFS for just that box and just the fields you tick, and displays the size before downloading.

  • Wind, gusts, pressure and waves as colours, barbs and labelled isobars over the chart.
  • A timeline to play the forecast forward.
  • Kept for offline use, warning when the forecast is getting old.
  • Tides at the nearest station, with the curve for the day.

Weather routing

Know when to leave.

Most weather routers plan one departure time. You still have to compare the days yourself.

Isomar routes your passage with the forecast, your boat's polar and the coastline. Then Best time tries every departure in a window and ranks them.

  • Departures ranked by their time at sea plus the hours upwind, under engine, in strong wind, gusts or rough sea, and at night, each weighted as you like.
  • Click the front to compare alternative routes, and play them all along the timeline.
  • Engine in light wind, wind limits, your own polar.

Performance

A polar that is more accurate

A router uses your boat's polar, and a designer's polar is for a new boat with new sails in flat water. Making your own is hard.

While you sail, Isomar keeps a log every 10 seconds: the instruments, the engine, and the forecast's wind and waves. It learns what your boat really does in each wind and gives you the option to update the polar.

  • Learns your polar from the wind instruments and speedo, where the log holds enough sailing, and keeps the polar you have everywhere else.
  • Finds your instruments' errors, such as a wind vane turned on its mount, a wind that seems to shift at every tack, or a speedo that reads low.
  • No wind instruments? With the GPS and the forecast's wind it tells you how fast the boat sails against its polar, for weather routing. A forecast is too imprecise to learn more so you will get an alert.
  • Knows when you motor from the engine revolutions in Signal K or NMEA, so time under engine never counts as sailing. You can also switch it manually.
  • Each weather route you follow to the end is compared with its plan.

Under way

Simple display with only useful instruments

At night or in traffic you need a few big, clear readouts and important alarms only.

Isomar switches to an under-way layout with big, readable instruments with ranked alerts.

  • NMEA 0183 over TCP, UDP or serial, gpsd and Signal K with data monitor to check that everything is correct.
  • AIS with CPA and TCPA collision alerts you can snooze or acknowledge.
  • Anchor watch, active route with cross-track error and ETA, track recording.

Phone and tablet

The same chart in your pocket.

Planning happens at the chart table, but the questions come on deck: where is that buoy, when does the wind back, how far to the anchorage?

Isomar runs on iPhone, iPad and Android with the same engine, charts and router as the desktop app, laid out for touch.

  • The pages as tabs in a sheet over the chart on a phone; a sidebar beside it on a tablet.
  • Weather routing and best departure on the phone itself, with the forecast downloaded for just your area.
  • Instruments from the boat over Wi-Fi (NMEA, gpsd, Signal K) or a USB serial adapter on Android, or the phone's own GPS.
  • Anchor watch and route keep running with the screen off, and the night palette dims the screen.

Charts and data

No subscription. No lock-in.

Chart apps that only open their own encrypted charts, on one device, for as long as you keep paying.

Isomar reads the charts you have licensed in open formats, and knows where the free ones are. The Charts page lists the free sources for the area on screen, rated for quality, and installs them in one click.

  • Free official charts where they exist, such as NOAA's for all US waters.
  • Your own folders of S-57 cells and KAP rasters.
  • GPX import and export compatible with many devices and software.
  • Everything stays on your computer: no account, no tracking.
